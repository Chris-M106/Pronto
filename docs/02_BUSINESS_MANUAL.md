## 2. What is Pronto?

**Pronto** is a two-sided marketplace for local home services in Tondela, Portugal.

**For customers:** Book a plumber, electrician, painter, or other service in 3 minutes. Payment protected. Job tracked in real-time. Problem solved.

**For providers:** Accept jobs based on your schedule. Get paid instantly. Build your reputation. No admin overhead.

**For us:** Take 18% commission on every completed job. Build the platform once, scale it across Portugal.

### Why "Pronto"?

- **Italian/Spanish for "quick"** — implies speed, action, getting things done
- **Works across languages** — not English-dependent
- **Founder resonance** — We believe in speed. Build fast, learn faster, iterate constantly
- **Brand personality** — Confident, reliable, no-nonsense

### The Core Insight

Portugal's home services market is entirely informal. There is no digital platform that combines:
1. **Payment protection** (escrow)
2. **Verified providers** (ID checked)
3. **Real reviews** (blind system, verified jobs only)
4. **Speed** (book in minutes, not days)
5. **Mobile-first** (not a desktop directory)

Every competitor either lacks one, or charges 25%+ commission. We do all five, at 18%.

## 3. The Problem We Solve

### For Customers

**The reality today:**
- Maria needs a plumber. She calls 3 numbers from Google and gets voicemail.
- She asks neighbors on WhatsApp. Someone recommends "my cousin's friend João" who does plumbing.
- João says he's available tomorrow but doesn't confirm. She texts him 3 times.
- He shows up 2 hours late. No written price. Charges €150 for a €60 job.
- No receipt. No recourse if something goes wrong next week.

**The pain:**
- No trust. How do I know he's qualified?
- No accountability. What if he doesn't show?
- No transparency. How much will this actually cost?
- No leverage. If something goes wrong, I have no recourse.

### For Providers

**The reality today:**
- João is a plumber with 20 years of experience. He's good at his job.
- But he spends 10 hours/week chasing leads via Facebook, WhatsApp, and word-of-mouth.
- His "reputation" is based on who he knows, not what he's actually delivered.
- When he gets a job, payment is often delayed or haggled.
- He has no way to scale beyond his immediate network.

**The pain:**
- Inconsistent work. Some weeks he's busy, some weeks he has nothing.
- Admin overhead. Invoice chasing, payment delays, no clear terms.
- No digital reputation. A plumber with 500 satisfied customers has no way to show it.
- High cost of acquisition. Ads, directories, word-of-mouth all cost time or money.

### The Market

**Size:** Portugal's informal home services market is worth €2.1 billion/year. Most transactions are cash, undocumented, uninsured.

**Fragmentation:** There are 340,000+ tradespeople in Portugal with no digital platform to aggregate them.

**Precedent:** TaskRabbit (US), Bark.com (UK), Handy (US/EU) all prove this problem is solvable. But none of them have launched aggressively in Portugal.

## 4. How We Make Money

### Revenue Model: Commission

**Simple:** We take 18% commission on every completed job.

**Example:** João completes a €100 plumbing job. We take €18. João gets €82.

**Why 18%?**
- **Below market:** TaskRabbit takes 25–40%. Bark takes 15% + lead fee. We're competitive.
- **Sustainable:** Enough to cover payment processing (2.9%), customer support, marketing, and platform costs.
- **Provider-friendly:** Only on completed jobs. No upfront fees. No hidden charges.
- **Customer-transparent:** Shown clearly at checkout.

## 6. Psychology of Trust

This is the most important section. Pronto works because we understand trust.

### Why Trust Matters

When Maria books João through Pronto, she's doing something she doesn't do on Facebook Marketplace:
- She's giving her address to a stranger
- She's inviting him into her home
- She's sharing sensitive information (what needs fixing)
- She's paying upfront (even in escrow)

This requires **deep trust.** We build it through:

### Trust Signal #1: Identity Verification

João has verified his ID through Stripe. Maria can see:
- ✅ His real name
- ✅ His face
- ✅ His age / location
- ✅ No criminal record (future: background check)

**Why it works:** Anonymous platforms fail because the provider could be anyone. Verification removes that fear.

### Trust Signal #2: Real Reviews

After João finishes, Maria leaves a review. But it's not just text — it's:
- ✅ Verified review (only people with completed bookings can review)
- ✅ His photo (not anonymous)
- ✅ Real names (both sides see each other)
- ✅ Specific rating (punctuality, quality, communication, value)

**Why it works:** Fake reviews (Airbnb problem) are nearly impossible. Reviews are tied to real, verified jobs.

### Trust Signal #3: Escrow Payment

Maria pays when she books. But her money doesn't go to João immediately. Instead:
1. Her card is charged
2. Money sits in escrow (held by Stripe)
3. João completes the job
4. Maria confirms completion (or auto-confirms in 24h)
5. Money released to João

**Why it works:** If João no-shows or does a bad job, Maria can dispute it and get her money back. This is protection that Facebook Marketplace doesn't offer.

### Trust Signal #4: Photo Evidence

João uploads a photo when he marks the job complete. Maria can see:
- ✅ Before/after (pipes fixed, paint applied, etc.)
- ✅ Proof of work
- ✅ Grounds for dispute if needed

**Why it works:** Makes it real. You can't fake a photo of a completed job.

### Trust Signal #5: Real Name, No Anonymous Ratings

Unlike Uber or Trustpilot, reviews on Pronto use:
- ✅ Real names (no "anonymous customer")
- ✅ Real jobs (you only see reviews from people who actually booked)
- ✅ No gaming (Pronto checks that reviewers actually paid for the job)

**Why it works:** Accountability cuts both ways. A bad review is credible because it comes from a real, verified person with a real job.

### Psychology: The "Pronto Guarantee"

We tell customers upfront:
> "If your provider no-shows, we'll find a replacement or give you a full refund within 2 hours. Period."

**Why it works:** Removes the biggest fear (flaking). Even if João cancels last-minute, Maria knows she's protected.

## 8. Protecting the Platform

Trust is our moat. So we protect it relentlessly.

### Fraud Prevention

| Risk | Prevention |
|------|-----------|
| **Fake providers** | ID verification + Stripe checks |
| **Fake reviews** | Only verified jobs can be reviewed + blind system |
| **Payment fraud** | Stripe Radar + velocity checks |
| **Bad behavior** | Rating drops below 3★ = automatic review + probation |
| **Scams** | Escrow holds money until completion proof |

### Customer Protection

- **Money-back guarantee** if provider no-shows
- **24-hour dispute window** after job completion
- **Admin review** of all disputes with photo evidence
- **Full refund** if fraud detected
- **Account access** to all transaction history

### Provider Protection

- **Fair review system** (both sides rate each other, blind until both submit)
- **Payment protection** (guaranteed payout once job confirmed)
- **Fast payouts** (instant or next business day)
- **No surprise fees** (only 18% commission, always transparent)
- **Probation system** (coaching + support if rating drops, not instant ban)

## 11. Legal & Tax in Portugal

### Business Structure

**Recommended:** Unipessoal (sole proprietor) OR Lda (limited company)

For Pronto, we recommend **Lda (Limitada)** because:
- Personal liability protection
- Easier to add co-founders/investors later
- More professional structure

**Cost:** €500–800 to incorporate

### Tax Implications

**Our take (18% commission):**
- Flat rate: 21% VAT on services + 28% IRC (corporate tax)
- Effective: ~28% of commission is taxes
- Example: €1,000 commission → €280 in taxes → €720 net

**Provider take (82% of job value):**
- Providers are self-employed
- Subject to IRS (income tax) + social contributions
- Typical: ~25% of income to taxes/contributions
- Pronto is NOT responsible for this

### Insurance & Liability

**Pronto's liability:**
- We don't employ providers (they're contractors)
- We're not liable if provider breaks customer's property
- BUT: We need platform liability insurance (€1,000–2,000/year)

**Provider liability:**
- Providers should have public liability insurance
- We'll recommend it in onboarding
- Not mandatory for MVP (legal risk, but necessary for scale)

### GDPR Compliance

**We must:**
- ✅ Have a privacy policy (by launch)
- ✅ Have Terms of Service (by launch)
- ✅ Store data securely (Supabase is EU-hosted)
- ✅ Honor "right to erasure" (allow users to delete their data)
- ✅ Have a DPA (Data Processing Agreement) with Stripe

**We must NOT:**
- ❌ Share customer data with 3rd parties without consent
- ❌ Store data outside EU (we don't — Frankfurt)
- ❌ Retain data longer than needed

### Payment Processing

**Stripe is compliant:**
- ✅ PCI DSS Level 1 (highest card data security)
- ✅ GDPR-compliant DPA
- ✅ Escrow model is transparent to customers
- ✅ Payout to providers is straightforward

## 16. How Could This Fail?

### Failure Scenario #1: Bad First Experience

**The risk:** First 10 customers have a bad experience (late provider, poor quality, payment issue).

**Why it kills:** Word-of-mouth in a 30K-person town spreads fast. Bad reviews sink startups.

**Mitigation:**
- Hand-pick first 30 providers (your personal network)
- Train them on Pronto's standards
- Manually oversee first 50 bookings
- Fix any issue within 24 hours (money back + personal call)

### Failure Scenario #2: Providers Don't Show Up

**The risk:** Providers join, but don't actively take jobs (engagement is low).

**Why it kills:** If providers aren't accepting jobs, customers see no one available. Platform becomes useless.

**Mitigation:**
- Daily calls with top 20 providers to understand blockers
- Weekly earnings reports (show them they're making money)
- Incentivize: extra featured placement for top performers
- Track: "acceptance rate" as top metric

### Failure Scenario #3: Customer Churn

**The risk:** First 100 customers try Pronto once, never come back.

**Why it kills:** You need repeat customers for unit economics to work (LTV is built on 4–5 jobs/customer).

**Mitigation:**
- Email campaigns: "Your trusted provider is still available"
- Loyalty points: 10% credit on every 5th booking
- "Rebook in 1 tap" button (make repeats frictionless)
- Monthly newsletter: "Services you need in May" (personalized suggestions)

### Failure Scenario #4: You Burn Out

**The risk:** Building this is exhausting. Personal network of providers, support calls, crisis management.

**Why it kills:** Startups die when founders give up, not when markets won't support them.

**Mitigation:**
- Find a co-founder (shared burden)
- Hire contractor for customer support (Month 2)
- Set boundaries (not 24/7 on-call)
- Weekly reflection (celebrate wins, not just problems)

### Failure Scenario #5: Stripe Dispute Rate Is Too High

**The risk:** Lots of customers dispute charges (claimed provider no-showed, did bad work).

**Why it kills:** High dispute rates trigger Stripe to freeze your account or shut you down.

**Mitigation:**
- Photo evidence (provider must upload completion photo)
- Dispute window (48h, not unlimited)
- Admin review (we manually decide, not algorithm)
- Communication (chat history proves expectations were set)
